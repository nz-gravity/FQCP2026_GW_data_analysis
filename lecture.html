Instructor run sheet — 120 minute lecture#

Instructor material. Deliberately kept out of _toc.yml, so it has a URL but does not appear in the student navigation.

Every beat below links into the built site. Open this page on the podium machine and drive the lecture from it.

Format#

No laptops in the room. Serve the built site and present it full screen:

uv run --locked python -m http.server -d _build/html 8000

Every figure and animation is already rendered, so nothing samples live in front of the room. Students get the Colab links at the end and run the labs themselves.

Exercises become predictions. Ask the question, take answers from the room, then scroll to the executed output. The *_answers pages are the reveal — open them in a second tab, never on screen before the question has been asked.

Before you walk in: open http://localhost:8000, zoom to ~150 %, check the back row can read a code cell, and click one animation to confirm it plays.

Closing and take-home

index

Eight modules do not fit in two hours. Modules 07, 08 and 10 are signposted in the closing block, not walked through. If you are running late, drop block 4 first — it is the shortest live route in the course and the easiest to summarise in two sentences.

Opening — 5 min#

\[\text{data} + \text{signal model} + \text{noise model} \to \text{likelihood} \to \text{posterior} \to \text{checks} \to \text{claim}.\]

Say the thing the whole course rests on: the data, the response and the noise model change from LVK to LISA; the logic in that line does not. Promise you will point at this map at the start of every block.

Block 1 — Bayes from scratch (25 min)#

01_bayesian_inference — stop at “End of the live route”.

  • The four pieces of Bayes’ theorem — stress that the model is chosen, not given.

  • Priors and prior predictive checks — draw from the prior, look at the data it implies. Cheapest bug-catcher in the course.

  • Tool 1: the posterior on a grid — exact, and it dies in dimension. That death is why samplers exist.

  • Tool 2: the posterior from a sampler — same posterior, no grid.

  • ASK: “Exercise 1 — I double the number of data points. What happens to the width of the posterior, and why?” Two answers from the room, then reveal.

  • Posterior predictive check, then the check that fails. Spend real time here: a posterior can be perfectly computed and still answer the wrong question.

  • The gravitational-wave bridge and from residuals to Whittle. This is the hinge of the course: everything after it is the same Gaussian likelihood with a harder forward model.

Skip in the room: evidence and Bayes factors, exercises 2–4. Name them as take-home.

Block 2 — LVK signals and matched filtering (20 min)#

02_lvk_signals_injections — stop at “End of the live route”.

  • Three questions that must stay separate — is there a signal / what is it / how do we know we are right. Every later confusion is one of these three collapsing into another.

  • ANIMATION: why chirp mass is measured so precisely. Best thirty seconds in the course for a non-specialist audience.

  • From source to a detector network.

  • Matched filtering, then ANIMATION: sliding the template through whitened data. Follow immediately with a template only works if it is close enough.

  • ASK: “how densely must a bank be packed?” Take the qualitative half only: if each extra parameter multiplies the template count, what does that do to the cost of a search? Arithmetic is take-home.

  • Inject and infer manually, then the same likelihood behind Bilby’s interface. Land the point: Bilby is an interface, not a new method.

Signpost only: sky localisation, distance–inclination degeneracy.

Block 3 — GW150914, end to end (20 min)#

03_lvk_gw150914_bilby. Attribute the GWOSC 5.2 tutorial out loud — source and attribution.

  • Get the analysis data, then why only four seconds and what the windows contain.

  • Estimate the PSD off-source, with why a median.

  • ASK: “why estimate the PSD away from the signal?” Short, everyone can answer — a good pace-recovery point if you are behind.

  • The restricted prior. Say plainly that the non-spinning model and fast sampler settings are workshop compromises, not the published analysis.

  • Run Dynesty, then compare with the published posterior.

  • ASK: “do the two posteriors agree — and what should ‘agree’ even mean: overlapping intervals, consistent medians, or matching widths?” (the question) Best discussion question in the course. Leave 3 minutes for it.

Buffer — 5 min#

Questions. If none come, this is where block 3’s overlay discussion runs long by design.

Block 4 — Populations and selection (10 min)#

04_lvk_population_and_checks — live route ends early; genuinely short.

  • Start with the catalogue you detected — and it is not the population.

  • The selection correction in one equation.

  • ASK: “the naive estimate is biased in a predictable direction — which direction?” Then reveal.

  • State the boundary: this is toy population inference, not production hierarchical inference. Events are posteriors, not numbers is take-home.

Block 5 — LISA: why it is different (15 min)#

05_lisa_signals_response_codes — stop at “End of the live route”.

  • Band and source zoo, then why LISA PE is unusually coupled — everything is on at once, forever.

  • ANIMATION: orbital motion becomes response modulation. Play it. This is the single idea that separates LISA from LVK in most people’s heads.

  • Sensitivity and Galactic confusion.

  • Inner product, SNR, and likelihood — point back at the opening map: same likelihood, harder response.

  • Analysis-code map — Erebor / Gemoo / GLASS / Eryn, one sentence each, so the names are not a mystery when they meet them in a talk.

Signpost only: links and delays into XYZ/AET, Fisher forecasts.

Block 6 — The global fit as Gibbs (15 min)#

06_lisa_global_fit_gibbs — stop at “End of the live route”.

  • The wheel of conditional analyses.

  • Gibbs versus blocked Metropolis-Hastings.

  • ANIMATION: one conditional block at a time. Narrate the shared residual being handed on — that hand-off is the global fit.

  • ASK: “why can one-pass subtraction depend on the order you remove sources in?” Answers first, then reveal.

  • Boundary: a fixed catalogue with BIC is not a trans-dimensional global fit.

Closing — 5 min#

Three things you did not walk through, one sentence and one figure each:

  • 07 — P-spline PSDs: the PSD is estimated inside the analysis, with uncertainty.

  • 08 — WDM time-frequency: gaps and non-stationarity; a diagonal WDM likelihood is an approximation once they induce covariance.

  • 10 — fast likelihoods: heterodyning and relative binning, and the accuracy check that decides whether you are allowed to use them.

Then: everything is on the site, every notebook opens in Colab with the rocket button and runs top to bottom on its own. Point at the blind data challenge as the thing to do if they only do one, and at the glossary and the literature list for what to read next. Close on the map you opened with.
